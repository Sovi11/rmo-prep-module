## The shape of the exam

RMO 2026 is on **Sunday 15 November, 1:00–4:00 pm**. Six problems, three hours, **102 marks — 17 per problem**, all carrying equal weight. Every recent paper carries the same line in the instructions:

> **No marks will be awarded for stating an answer without justification.**

That single sentence is the difference between IOQM and RMO, and it is the thing to internalise first. In IOQM you scored 50 by getting numbers right. Here, a correct number with no argument scores **zero**, and a complete argument for three problems beats scrappy work on all six.

## How the arithmetic of marks works

Three complete solutions is 51/102. In most regions and years that is comfortably in the qualifying range. Six half-solutions is often worth less than one complete one, because partial credit at RMO is genuinely partial — you get marks for *reaching a stated milestone*, not for effort.

So the strategy the whole plan is built around:

1. **Read all six problems in the first five minutes.** Rank them by how likely you are to *finish*.
2. **Finish your best two before touching a third.** Write them out completely, then move on.
3. Only then spend what is left going deep on a third and fourth.

## How to actually work through a chapter

Read the theory once, properly, with a pen. Then go straight to the problems. **Give every problem 25 minutes of real thought before opening the hint**, and another 15 after the hint before opening the solution. A problem you struggle with for 40 minutes and then read is worth ten you skim.

Use the status button on each problem honestly:

- **Attempted** — you worked on it and did not finish.
- **Stuck** — you had no route at all. This is the most useful label; it points at a missing tool.
- **Solved with hint** — you finished, but needed the nudge. Come back to it in a fortnight.
- **Solved** — you finished it alone, and you could write it up cleanly right now.

Flag (⚑) anything that surprised you. Week 9 is built out of those flags.

## One habit that matters more than any theorem

**Write at least two solutions in full, on paper, every week**, as if they were being marked. Not notes, not a sketch — complete sentences, stated claims, a visible conclusion. Most people who understand enough mathematics to qualify for RMO do not qualify, and the reason is almost always that they never practised writing.
